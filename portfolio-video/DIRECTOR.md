# Director's Brief — "Heartbeat Signal"

## What the viewer should feel
**Curiosity → credibility → warmth → invitation**, in 15 seconds.

1. **Curiosity (0–1.5 s).** A lab instrument and a heartbeat. "Who builds AI… matters." It's a question,
   so the viewer leans in.
2. **Recognition (1.5–3.5 s).** A real person, rendered as a solid 3D object rather than a flat title. Weight.
3. **Credibility (3.5–11.5 s).** Five proofs at a quickening pace. Each one is a *specific* number or
   artifact, not an adjective. The A3 beat is the slowest, because it is the most original: it *shows*
   the sub-pixel problem being solved, with real computed values, rather than claiming it.
4. **Warmth (11.5–13.5 s).** The only centered, slow, serif moment. The machine voice gives way to the
   human voice: "for Good Faith of Humanity".
5. **Invitation (13.5–15 s).** The heartbeat returns calmer, the name settles, and the door is open.

## Shot intent
- **The beam is the protagonist.** If a frame doesn't show the beam, it is cut on the beam's pulse.
  The bottom "beam rail" runs through the whole montage.
- **Numbers are the actors** in the proof beats. They enter with weight (slam, back.out) and leave fast.
  Captions support the numbers; they never compete with them.
- **Precision is the aesthetic.** It's an instrument, not a nightclub: hairline graticules, mono readouts, exact
  values. Glow is localized and earned (the beam, the hero number).
- **Rhythm.** Lub-dub every beat. Cuts land on the "lub". Slams land on the "dub".

## What it must never look like
- A generic AI video: centered text on a gradient, cyan neon, floating cards.
- A slideshow: equal-weight scenes with fade-in-from-below on every element.
- Inflated. Every number comes from the BRIEF's Facts table.
